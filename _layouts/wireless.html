MGY · April 14–15, 1912

The Wireless Log

Every known transmission sent and received by MGY from the collision with the iceberg at 11:40pm on April 14th 1912, to the final fading signal at 2:17am on April 15th. Sourced from testimony given to the British and American inquiries, 1912.

Ice Warnings — April 14, 1912
9:00am
Caronia → MGY
Captain Barr reports bergs, growlers and field ice ahead.
1:42pm
Baltic → MGY
Reports icebergs and large quantities of field ice in vicinity.
9:40pm
Mesaba → MGY
"In lat 42N to 41.25N long 49W to 50.30W saw much heavy pack ice and great number of large icebergs also field ice. Weather good, clear."
⚠ Never passed to the bridge.
10:55pm
Californian → MGY
"We are stopped and surrounded by ice."
⚠ Phillips replies: "Shut up, shut up, I am busy working Cape Race." Californian's operator closes down for the night.
Distress Transmissions — April 14–15, 1912
11:40pm
—
Titanic strikes iceberg. Captain Smith orders Phillips to send distress call.
12:15am
MGY → All stations
CQD MGY — Position 41.44N 50.24W — Require assistance. First distress call sent. Received by La Provence, Mount Temple and Cape Race.

12:25am
MGY → Carpathia
"Come at once. We have struck a berg. It's a CQD O.M. Position 41.46N 50.14W"
12:26am
Carpathia → MGY
"Shall I tell my Captain? Do you require assistance?"
12:26am
MGY → Carpathia
"Yes, come quick!"
12:27am
MGY → Ypiranga
"CQD here. Corrected position 41.46N 50.14W. Require immediate assistance. We have collision with iceberg. Sinking. Can nothing hear for noise of steam." Sent 15–20 times.
12:30am
MGY → Frankfurt
"Tell your Captain to come to our help. We are on the ice."
12:36am
Frankfurt → MGY
"What is the matter with u?"
12:36am
MGY → Frankfurt
"We have collision with iceberg. Sinking. Please tell Captain to come."
12:40am
MGY → Carpathia
"SOS Titanic sinking by the head. We are about all down. Sinking."
12:45am
MGY → Olympic
SOS — First use of SOS by Titanic. Bride jokingly suggests to Phillips it may be his last chance to use the new international distress signal.
1:00am
MGY → Olympic
"We have struck an iceberg."
1:02am
MGY → Asian
"Want immediate assistance."
1:10am
MGY → Olympic
"We are in collision with berg. Sinking head down. 41.46N 50.14W. Come soon as possible."
1:10am
MGY → Olympic
Captain Smith: "Get your boats ready. What is your position?"
1:25am
MGY → Olympic
"We are putting the women off in the boats."
1:30am
MGY → Olympic
"We are putting passengers off in small boats."
1:35am
MGY → Olympic
Weather report: "Clear and calm."
1:35am
MGY → Baltic
"Engine room getting flooded."
1:45am
MGY → Carpathia
"Engine-room full up to boilers."
Last signals heard by Carpathia.
2:00am
MGY → All
Virginian hears Titanic calling very faintly. Power greatly reduced.
2:10am
MGY → All
Virginian hears two V's signalled faintly. Probably adjusting spark.
2:17am
MGY → All
Virginian hears Titanic call CQ. Signals end very abruptly as power suddenly switched off. Spark blurred and ragged. No further response from MGY.
Final transmission from MGY.
2:20am
—
Titanic founders. 41.46N 50.14W. 1,503 souls lost.
Source: Titanic Inquiry Project, compiled by Rob Ottmers. British and American Inquiry transcripts, 1912.
